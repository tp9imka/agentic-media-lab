Use the order, not the decimals: when a cut breaks continuity but keeps emotion and story, keep it. The book itself was not opened. Other secondary pages seen in search give different percentages for the lower four items; this page matches the most common set.

## Claims

1. Murch weights emotion above the other five criteria combined.

   > Ultimately he says that emotion is worth more than the other 5 combined, since if the emotion is right it will mask any other deficiencies.

   Locator: paragraph after the six criteria. Source: <https://cloud.wikis.utexas.edu/wiki/spaces/rtf318/pages/80022510/Murch+s+Rule+of+Six>, retrieved 2026-11-12.

2. The weights are emotion 51%, story 23%, rhythm 10% and eye-trace 7%, with 5% and 4% for 2D and 3D continuity.

   > Emotion – 51% Story – 23% Rhythm – 10% Eye-trace – 7%

   Locator: list Rule of Six: When to Cut. Source: <https://cloud.wikis.utexas.edu/wiki/spaces/rtf318/pages/80022510/Murch+s+Rule+of+Six>, retrieved 2026-11-12.

3. When something has to give, sacrifice from the bottom of the list up.

   > Murch suggests that if you must sacrifice to make a cut you start from the bottom of this list and work your way up

   Locator: paragraph after the six criteria. Source: <https://cloud.wikis.utexas.edu/wiki/spaces/rtf318/pages/80022510/Murch+s+Rule+of+Six>, retrieved 2026-11-12.
